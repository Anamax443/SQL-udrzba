1The goal and how it is measured

The stated goal: be able to restore the company to any point in time with the least possible loss of work. Two numbers measure it.

MetricMeaningBeforeNowTarget
RPOhow much work we lose15 min by day
up to 13 h 44 min at night		15 min Sun–Fri5 min in working hours
RTOhow long the business is downnot measurednot measured< 4 h (to be confirmed by management)
Where the 13 h 44 min came from The last log backup ran at 15:46, the next at 05:30. Throughout that window, all work performed existed in a single copy — the file NAV_LIVE_Log.ldf on drive F:. Losing that volume overnight meant losing everything since 15:46.

What RPO does not limit: the precision of a restore. The 05:30 backup also contained every transaction since 15:46, so restoring to 02:00 at night was possible via STOPAT. Granularity is to the second regardless of frequency. Frequency decides only how much work exists in one copy only.

Proof of the cap The newer export reaches further into the morning, yet its oldest record is 2.35 seconds younger than the older export's. Eleven newer events (8 job failures + 3 GPO) pushed exactly eleven of the oldest 9002 errors out of the window: 286 − 275 = 11.

The fix worked, but it exposed a second layer of the problem.

Where the 140 GB is produced

hour  0–3     ~0.7 GB total
hour  4      94.59 GB   ← 1xdenne
hour  5      45.99 GB   ← 1xdenne
hour  6–18   ~0.3–0.5 GB/h   (normal load, 57 users)
hour 19–22   ~4.4 GB total
                          total ~151 GB/day

93 % of the daily log volume is generated between 04:00 and 06:00. The source is job 1xdenne (starts 04:00, runs 1 h 46 min), whose step 1 update_index performs index maintenance.

🔴 Why more frequent backups will not fix this The 05:00 backup took 3 seconds — it released almost nothing — yet twelve minutes later the log was full. The log was held by a long open transaction: a single ALTER INDEX … REBUILD is one transaction, and until it commits, no backup returns its space. The following backup at 05:31 then carried 29.43 GB at once.

Shortening the interval to 5 minutes would therefore not help at all. Only a higher log cap (buffer) and a change to index maintenance itself will.

What step 1 of 1xdenne does

DECLARE @PROCENT AS int = 10;
...
'ALTER INDEX [' + i.name + '] ON [...] REBUILD;'
WHERE ips.avg_fragmentation_in_percent > @PROCENT
AND ips.page_count > 100
#FindingImpact
1Threshold 10 % and a single action — full rebuildprimary source of the 140 GB
2page_count > 100 (800 kB) instead of the usual 1000a lot of unnecessary small work
3UPDATE STATISTICS on top, once per index of the same tablepure waste — REBUILD updates statistics itself
4Errors swallowed into a table variablethe job reports OK even if half of it fails
5No SORT_IN_TEMPDB, no MAXDOPsorting inside NAV-LIVE, unbounded parallelism

The proposed replacement is in sql/1xdenne-krok1-udrzba-indexu-navrh.sql — tiered logic (5–30 % REORGANIZE, > 30 % REBUILD), a time budget, and RAISERROR on failure. It has a @JenVypis = 1 switch that prints the plan without executing anything.

A common misconception ONLINE = ON does not reduce log volume — it adds a version store. It helps availability, not the log. The fragmentation threshold is the real lever.

3Root cause — why the log fills up

The one sentence the whole document rests on Under FULL recovery, a transaction log backup is the only mechanism that frees space inside the log. It is not optional protection — it is maintenance. 
transaction → written to log → space is not freed → log grows
   → log hits its cap → SQL rejects writes → error 9002
   → nightly processing fails → morning spent finding out what didn't run

The error message states it precisely:

The transaction log for database 'NAV-LIVE' is full due to 'LOG_BACKUP'.

due to 'LOG_BACKUP' does not mean "the disk is full". It means "the log cannot be reused because it is waiting for a log backup that never came."

SELECT log_reuse_wait_desc FROM sys.databases WHERE name = 'NAV-LIVE';
ValueMeaning
NOTHINGhealthy, the log has space to recycle
LOG_BACKUPwaiting for a log backup — our case
ACTIVE_TRANSACTIONheld by a long open transaction
ACTIVE_BACKUP_OR_RESTOREa backup is running (transient, normal)

A transient value other than NOTHING is legitimate. Alert only on a state lasting more than 60 minutes.

4The original design intent

The previous configuration was not an oversight. It followed from a deliberate line of reasoning that must be recorded so nobody later mistakes it for a mistake:

  1. Data is entered by people, during working hours on working days; at weekends only exceptionally.
  2. At night, reports are generated, and those can be regenerated.
  3. Therefore what needs protecting is chiefly what people entered.

That reasoning remains the basis of the design. The distinction between daytime and nightly data is reflected in the interval of backups. Three points, however, diverge from how SQL Server actually works:

#DivergenceExplanation
aA nightly log backup is not protection of nightly dataIt is maintenance — without it the log is never freed and nothing runs in the morning. See section 3.
bThe previous configuration saved nothingLog volume does not change with backup frequency. The same ~140 GB is backed up either way — spread out, or in one block at 05:30. Same space, same retention.
cThe window ended at 15:46, not after working hoursAn order entered at 16:20 was not backed up until 05:30 the next morning. That is exactly the kind of data the design most wants to protect.
Still to verify Is the nightly workload really only regenerable reports? According to the demilitarisation script for NAV-TEST, the job queue also handles EDI, M-Files and webshop sync. An EDI document already sent to a partner cannot be regenerated — nor can a posted document.

Log shipping is not a backup It is built on log backups and adds fast failover for hardware failure. User error, ransomware and corrupted data are all replicated within minutes. The correct framing is backups + optionally log shipping, never "instead of".

6Rollout procedure

  1. Raise the log cap — do this tonight seconds · metadata only · safe during production 
    USE [master];
GO
ALTER DATABASE [NAV-LIVE] MODIFY FILE (NAME = N'NAV_LIVE_Log', MAXSIZE = 250GB);
GO
USE [NAV-LIVE];
SELECT name,
       CAST(size*8.0/1024/1024 AS decimal(10,1))     AS size_gb,
       CAST(max_size*8.0/1024/1024 AS decimal(10,1)) AS max_gb
FROM sys.database_files WHERE type_desc = 'LOG';

    max_gb must read 250.0 and size_gb stays 97.7 — the file does not grow now, only its ceiling moves. It will grow at night in 4 GB increments; each increment is zero-initialised (Instant File Initialization does not apply to log files), causing brief pauses. Far better than error 9002.

  2. Add Saturday to the log backup schedule one checkbox · reversible

    SQL Agent → Jobs → BackupMaintenancePlan.Tlog → Schedules → tick Saturday.

    SELECT j.name, s.freq_interval,
       STUFF(STUFF(RIGHT('000000'+CAST(s.active_start_time AS varchar(6)),6),5,0,':'),3,0,':') AS start_,
       STUFF(STUFF(RIGHT('000000'+CAST(s.active_end_time AS varchar(6)),6),5,0,':'),3,0,':')   AS end_,
       s.date_modified
FROM msdb.dbo.sysjobs j
JOIN msdb.dbo.sysjobschedules js ON js.job_id = j.job_id
JOIN msdb.dbo.sysschedules s ON s.schedule_id = js.schedule_id
WHERE j.name = 'BackupMaintenancePlan.Tlog';

    freq_interval must become 127 (all seven days: Sun 1 + Mon 2 + Tue 4 + Wed 8 + Thu 16 + Fri 32 + Sat 64). Verify from data — a GUI change on 2026-08-10 silently failed to save once already.

  3. Review the index maintenance plan — read-only 15 min · changes nothing

    Run sql/1xdenne-krok1-udrzba-indexu-navrh.sql with @JenVypis = 1. It prints what it would do and how many GB fall into each category. Only then replace the contents of step 1 of job 1xdenne.

  4. Notifications on backup jobs 3 statements · reversible 
    EXEC msdb.dbo.sp_update_job @job_name = N'BackupMaintenancePlan.Tlog',
     @notify_level_email = 2, @notify_email_operator_name = N'AdminOperatorRobot';
EXEC msdb.dbo.sp_update_job @job_name = N'BackupMaintenancePlan.Tlog2',
     @notify_level_email = 2, @notify_email_operator_name = N'AdminOperatorRobot';
EXEC msdb.dbo.sp_update_job @job_name = N'Backup_NAV-LIVE_for_TEST_Optimized.Subplan_1',
     @notify_level_email = 2, @notify_email_operator_name = N'AdminOperatorRobot';

    Database Mail works (profile SQLMailForNotification, verified by test message). The reason nobody noticed months of failures is that these jobs have no operator assigned.

  5. Daily verification 2 min · read-only 
    SELECT DATEPART(HOUR, backup_finish_date) AS hour_, COUNT(*) AS cnt,
       CAST(SUM(backup_size)/1024.0/1024/1024 AS decimal(10,2)) AS gb
FROM msdb.dbo.backupset
WHERE database_name='NAV-LIVE' AND type='L'
  AND backup_finish_date > DATEADD(DAY,-1,GETDATE())
GROUP BY DATEPART(HOUR, backup_finish_date) ORDER BY hour_;

8Recovery procedures

8.1 User error — return to a point in time

Never restore over production Point-in-time recovery is always whole-database. Rolling NAV-LIVE back to 09:59 discards the work of all 57 users since. Restore a copy under a different name and move the data across manually. 
RESTORE DATABASE [NAV-RECOVER]
FROM DISK = 'G:\Backup\...\NAV-LIVE_full.bak'
WITH MOVE 'NAV_LIVE_Data' TO 'T:\Database\NAV-RECOVER\NAV-RECOVER.mdf',
     MOVE 'NAV_LIVE_Log'  TO 'T:\Database\NAV-RECOVER\NAV-RECOVER.ldf',
     NORECOVERY, STATS = 5;

RESTORE LOG [NAV-RECOVER] FROM DISK = '...\log_001.trn' WITH NORECOVERY;
-- ...
RESTORE LOG [NAV-RECOVER] FROM DISK = '...\log_NNN.trn'
WITH RECOVERY, STOPAT = '2026-08-10T09:59:00';

8.2 When the exact time is unknown — STANDBY

Between individual RESTORE LOG steps the database can be inspected, letting you close in on the moment of the error step by step:

RESTORE LOG [NAV-RECOVER] FROM DISK = '...\log_005.trn'
WITH STANDBY = 'T:\Database\NAV-RECOVER\undo.dat',
     STOPAT = '2026-08-10T09:00:00';

SELECT TOP 10 * FROM [NAV-RECOVER].dbo.[AXIMA$Sales Header] ORDER BY [No_] DESC;

8.3 Database lost, server alive

  1. Tail-log backup first, while the .ldf is readable — this reduces the loss to virtually zero: 
    BACKUP LOG [NAV-LIVE] TO DISK = 'G:\Backup\tail.trn' WITH NORECOVERY, INIT;
    If the log is damaged: WITH CONTINUE_AFTER_ERROR. This is the only legitimate use of that option.
  2. Full → (differential) → all logs → tail log WITH RECOVERY
  3. Restart the BC service on B-S-W-NAV-01

8.4 Whole server lost

master and msdb must be restored as well — without them, logins, jobs and maintenance plans are rebuilt by hand. They are small and must be part of the backup plan. Order: install SQL Server → master (single-user) → msdb → NAV-LIVE.

8.5 Acceptance protocol — when recovery is done

Recovery is not complete merely because the database is ONLINE:

  • Database is ONLINE and DBCC CHECKDB is clean
  • BC service tier has connected to the database
  • A user has signed in to the Business Central client
  • The last posted document matches the expected recovery time
  • A test sales invoice posts without number-series errors
  • Job queue is Ready, nothing stuck in In Process
  • M-Files and EDI folders are functional
  • Recorded in the operations log — what was restored, why, and to where

8.6 Restore testing

Restore-NAV-TEST.ps1 restores NAV-TEST from the latest .bak and demilitarises it. Every run is proof that the full backup is restorable and a measurement of RTO. Three things are missing:

  1. Record the outcome and duration — otherwise there is no measured RTO and no history.
  2. Once a month, restore with STOPAT to a random time from the previous week. Today's test verifies only the full backup, not the log chain — precisely the part the whole goal rests on.
  3. DBCC CHECKDB on the restored copy — integrity checking for free, without touching production.
A bug in the script 
Sort-Object LastAccessTime -Descending
Last-access-time updates are disabled by default on modern Windows (NtfsDisableLastAccessUpdate). The script may pick an old backup; the restore succeeds and nobody notices that week-old data is being tested. Use LastWriteTime and add an age check.
Security of the test copy Restore-NAV-TEST.ps1 produces a full copy of live company data every day. Demilitarisation disconnects the surrounding systems — not access to the data itself. Anyone with access to NAV-TEST has all company data, one day old. This may be a consciously accepted risk; it must at least be recorded.
